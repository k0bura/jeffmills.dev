## Context

Even with a strong design system, a major gap remained. Meridian primarily served developers. Business users (marketing and content teams) relied on AEM. Engineers built reusable components, but business users couldn't easily use them. The result was slower content delivery, inconsistent experiences, and continued reliance on engineering for work that should have been self-service.

The front-end platform needed to connect to the business content ecosystem as the next stage of its value.

## My role

I led the Meridian-to-AEM integration. I designed the technical model, influenced how components are delivered inside AEM, and positioned Meridian as a business enablement platform rather than just a developer system.

## What I did

**Integration model.** I established it. The Meridian UI library is deployed into AEM, and AEM templates consume Meridian web components directly. The rule is that Meridian owns the components and AEM declares them in templates. AEM stays thin, and the design system stays the source of truth. Because AEM only declares the components, every touchpoint stays consistent.

**Business use of engineering assets.** I integrated Meridian components into AEM so marketers and content creators use pre-built components without needing custom engineering. The phrase that came out of the rollout was *put the design system at the fingertips of the business*.

**Consistency across channels.** The same Meridian components power applications, marketing pages, and content experiences. That is one visual layer, and it stays consistent across channels without a separate effort to enforce it.

**Content delivery at scale.** Business teams build pages from reusable components and deliver content faster, without engineering bottlenecks.

**Design, engineering, and content flow.** I aligned the Meridian component library, the Figma design system, and the AEM templates to a single mental model. Nothing gets re-interpreted between stages, and no component has duplicate implementations for different audiences.

## Resistance

**AEM architecture complexity.** AEM has its own component model and rendering constraints. Integrating a modern design system into AEM was hard and needed a clean abstraction.

**Organizational boundaries.** Engineering built and marketing requested. Moving to marketing self-service via Meridian took trust, new workflows, and behavior change on multiple sides.

**Competing approaches.** Other groups explored different AEM component architectures. Without a clear shared direction, the experience would have fragmented across Vanguard.

## How I handled it

I simplified the integration model into a single principle, *Meridian owns components, AEM consumes them*, which removed the ambiguity that usually kills cross-system integrations. I framed the work in business outcomes (speed to market, consistency, scalability) instead of technical elegance. I drove alignment through practical implementation, demos, walkthroughs, and spreading the word.

## Outcome

- **Business enablement at scale.** Marketing and content teams use Meridian components directly in AEM. Content work depends less on engineering.
- **Faster content delivery.** Reusable components mean faster page creation, and templated content no longer needs custom builds. The organization moves faster without scaling engineering cost linearly.
- **Cross-channel consistency.** Apps, AEM pages, and digital experiences use the same components. Non-engineers can ship on-brand digital experiences.
- **Stronger ROI from Meridian.** Meridian is no longer a developer tool. It is a business platform with measurable impact.
- **Foundation for future work.** This integration is what makes personalization at scale, AI-driven content generation, and dynamic component-driven experiences possible.
- **Same primitives as Chapter 3.** The component primitives that supported multi-framework apps in Chapter 3 now serve marketing and content. That is the payoff of building framework-agnostic from the start.
