## Context

Delphic Digital was a boutique Philadelphia agency. The work was front-end engineering for enterprise clients across CMS platforms most engineers never touch in one place: WordPress, Drupal, Sitecore, custom C#, PHP. The brands rotated: Sunrise Senior Living one quarter, The Borgata the next, then Comcast, Wolftrap, and others. Each one shipped in six-to-twelve-week windows with its own visual language, its own integration constraints, and its own QA bar.

My job was the *system* underneath all of those projects: what we reused, what we standardized, what we treated as agency-wide infrastructure, and what we let each project decide. That&rsquo;s the lens I came out of agency life with, and I&rsquo;ve carried it since.

Before Delphic, I spent seven years (2004-2011) at smaller Philadelphia shops: Singularity Design, Whittman-Hart, and Vision3 Inc., where I was the first engineering hire. I did award-winning interactive work for clients including Centocor and St. Jude. Those years are where I learned to ship. Delphic is where I took on lead roles.

## Roles at Delphic

**Sr. Front End Developer (Nov 2011, June 2014).** These were my individual-contributor years. I built front-end features for major brands during the mobile-web transition, the responsive shift, the death of pixel-perfect comps, and the rise of JavaScript as more than a polyfill. I spent two and a half years shipping under client deadlines while the industry rewrote itself.

**Lead Front End Developer (June 2014, Feb 2016).** This is where my work changed. I set front-end architecture for client projects, defined technical standards, and contributed deeply to implementation. The work that mattered most was the **internal front-end tooling and shared libraries to accelerate delivery across the team**, more than the per-project code. That posture became my career.

**Director of Front End Development (Feb 2016, April 2017).** I led the front-end practice. I architected systems across CMSes for enterprise clients, owned planning from concept through deployment, and **established front-end development standards across the agency**, while staying hands-on in code reviews and complex implementations. I was a hands-on technical lead from the start.

Across the three roles, my work went from "I write the code" to "I write the system that other engineers write code against."

## The Skeletor framework

The most distinctive thing from those years is the **Skeletor framework**, a multi-repo agency front-end system I helped build out from 2014 to 2017. By 2016 it was a set of focused libraries: a runtime, a component loader, Sass token systems, a debug layer, and a documentation site. By late 2016 it had grown into native Web Components, with components extending <code>HTMLElement</code> directly. That was before the Web Components v1 spec stabilized in 2018 and before Lit existed.

Skeletor used these patterns:

- **Standards-based components.** Components were primitives built on web standards and independent of any framework.
- **A small shared runtime** with feature detection, debug helpers, a component registry, and Sass-driven design tokens.
- **Accessibility as a baseline.** A sister `accessible-components` library wired WAI-ARIA attributes at component init, so nothing waited for an audit pass afterward.
- **Documentation alongside code.** A custom docs site was the precursor to every Storybook setup I&rsquo;ve built since.

The code is on the [Skeletor project page](/projects/skeletor), including the actual `extends HTMLElement` source from December 2016 and the multi-era arc from a 2013 responsive utility through a 2017 design-system pattern library. The agency archived the org, but the architectural posture is in production today as Meridian.

## What carried over

Three things, in order of how they show up in everything I&rsquo;ve done since:

**Breadth.** I worked five years across different verticals (healthcare, hospitality, casino, performing arts, telecom), different CMSes, and different brand systems. That range is hard to fake. Every enterprise project I&rsquo;ve led at Vanguard has been faster because I&rsquo;d already seen the integration shape, just with different vendors. In my experience, agency-trained engineers approach platform problems with a fluency that single-stack engineers take years to build.

**Skills that transferred.** I was Director of Front End Development before Vanguard&rsquo;s lead role, accountable for the practice across all client engagements. I&rsquo;ve been at Vanguard since 2017. The skills transferred without modification: setting standards, mentoring under deadline pressure, deciding what&rsquo;s reusable and what&rsquo;s project-specific, and hiring. The Vanguard work scaled up, but the shape of the job didn&rsquo;t change.

**Platform thinking.** It has to start before there&rsquo;s a platform. The Skeletor framework existed because the agency couldn&rsquo;t afford to reinvent the front end on every client engagement. That same instinct to build the substrate and not the moment made the Meridian work possible nine years later. Meridian is an upgrade of the same posture. The framework-agnostic instincts, the platform-first posture, and the discipline around shared tooling all got their first reps at Delphic.
